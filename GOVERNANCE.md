# Governance

Docqet is open source and maintained by [Exalynt](https://github.com/exalynt).
It uses a **benevolent dictator governance model**, a common and
well-understood structure in open-source projects.

## What this means

- **Anyone can propose ideas, designs and code.** Discussions, issues and
  pull requests from any contributor are taken seriously and judged on their
  merits.
- **Day-to-day decisions are delegated.** Maintainers review pull requests,
  triage issues, and decide the technical design within their areas, so
  Docqet doesn't wait on one person.
- **Final direction rests with the project lead.**
  [John Peterson](https://github.com/john-peterson-g17) leads Docqet for
  Exalynt and has final say over its direction and implementation, including
  overriding a delegated decision when necessary.

## Decisions Docqet keeps for its maintainers

Docqet's own principles apply to how Docqet is run:

- **Stability levels** of Docqet's capabilities are set by maintainers.
- **Changes to the format** are proposed in the open and decided by
  maintainers, because every project that uses Docqet depends on them.
- **Open decisions** are recorded in
  [Docqet's own docs](https://github.com/docqet/docqet/tree/main/docqet)
  until they're settled, and settled in a reviewed pull request.
- **Intention an agent writes** counts only once a person confirms it.

## Why this model

One point of final authority keeps Docqet coherent while it's young: it
avoids design by committee, settles disagreements that don't converge on
their own, and keeps the format consistent for everyone who builds on it.

In practice, it's used sparingly. Most decisions are made together, through
the review and discussion described in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).

This model can change as the community and the maintainers grow. A change to
Docqet's governance would go through the same open discussion as any other
significant decision.
